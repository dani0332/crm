🔧 Sage Integration - Booking Process

Complete Guide to Policy Booking in Sage 300 ERP System

Active Documentation

❓ What is This Documentation About?

This documentation explains how our insurance brokerage system integrates with Sage 300 ERP to book insurance policies. When a customer purchases a policy, we need to record financial transactions (customer payments, insurer premiums, commissions) in Sage 300 for proper accounting and financial reporting.

💡 Why Sage 300 Integration?

Sage 300 is an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system that manages our company's finances. When a policy is sold, we must:

✓ Record the customer's payment (Accounts Receivable - AR)
✓ Record what we owe to the insurance company (Accounts Payable - AP)
✓ Track our commission earnings
✓ Maintain accurate financial records for auditing and reporting

This integration automates these accounting entries, ensuring accuracy and compliance.

🔍 Step 1: Pre-Booking Validation Checks

Before we can book a policy to Sage 300, we must verify that everything is ready and correct. These validation checks prevent errors, duplicate bookings, and ensure data integrity.

These checks happen inside the postBookPolicyToSage() function in SageApiService.php

⚠️ Why Are These Checks Critical?

Failing any of these checks will STOP the booking process. This prevents:
• Duplicate financial records in Sage 300
• Booking when the accounting system is unavailable
• Processing policies with missing or invalid payment information
• Creating inconsistencies between our system and Sage 300

🚦 Core System Checks

These checks ensure the system is ready and the policy hasn't been processed before.

1. Policy Not Already Booked Checks: quote_status_id != PolicyBooked
Why: Prevents duplicate bookings that would create incorrect financial records
2. Sage System Enabled Checks: isSageEnabled() == true
Why: Ensures Sage 300 integration is active and configured before attempting connection
3. Embedded Product Ready Checks: isEmbeddedTransactionStatusReadyForSage()
Why: Verifies add-on products (MDX, RDX, ECB) are in correct status for booking
4. Valid Quote Type Checks: QuoteTypes::getIdFromValue()
Why: Confirms the policy type (Motor, Health, Travel) is recognized and valid

💳 Payment Validation Logic

Payment must exist and be valid before we can record financial transactions in Sage.

1. Payment Record Exists Payment::where('code', $quote->code)->mainLeadPayment()
Why: We must have a payment record to create receipts and invoices
2. Duplicate Lead Handling Check parent_duplicate_quote_id for payment resolution
Why: If this is a duplicate quote, use the parent quote's payment instead
3. Credit Card Payment Status Unpaid CC payments with INSURER collection type
Why: Insurer-collected payments must be confirmed before Sage booking
4. Zero Price Policies total_price == 0 requires CreditApproval method
Why: Free policies need special approval method (no actual payment to record)

🏢 Special Case: Health AUH Lead Processing

Abu Dhabi Health Authority (AUH) leads follow a different process and skip Sage booking.

1. AUH Lead Detection isAHisHealthAUHLead() check for special processing
Why: AUH leads are handled directly by the health authority, not through Sage
2. Document Sent Verification isPolicySentLogExists() for customer documents
Why: Confirms policy documents were sent to customer before marking complete
3. Status Update Direct PolicyBooked status for AUH leads
Why: Mark as booked without Sage integration for AUH leads
4. Payment Processing straightforwardPayments() for AUH leads
Why: AUH handles their own accounting; simplified payment processing

👤 1. Customer Verification & Creation

What it does: Before we can record any financial transactions in Sage 300, the customer must exist in the accounting system. This process automatically checks if the customer already exists, and if not, creates a new customer record with proper numbering and categorization.

Real-world example: When John Smith buys car insurance, we first check if "John Smith" exists in Sage. If not, we create customer "P00001" (P for Personal) with his details.

  • Automatic customer existence verification
  • Smart customer numbering system (P/C prefixes)

  • Database synchronization

🧾 2. Prepayment Receipt Creation

What it does: Records the customer's payment in Sage 300 as a "prepayment receipt" - note that prepayment and receipt are the SAME thing in Sage, just different terminology. This creates a payment record that will later be matched to the invoice.

Real-world example: Customer pays AED 2,500 for their policy on Monday. We immediately create a prepayment receipt showing "Customer paid AED 2,500". On Tuesday when we generate their invoice, we'll "apply" (link) this receipt to the invoice to close the transaction.

  • AR Receipts: Customer payments received
  • AP Receipts: Our payments to insurance companies
  • NRA Case: Receipts created AFTER policy booking
  • Split payment & installment receipt handling
  • Open → Ready to Post → Posted (3-step workflow)
  • Database: sage_reciept_id stores receipt reference
View Prepayment Receipt Creation →

📄 3. AR Invoice Lifecycle

What it does: Creates the customer's invoice in Sage 300 showing what they owe us (Accounts Receivable). This includes the premium they must pay to the insurance company AND our brokerage commission.

Real-world example: Customer's car insurance costs AED 2,300 (premium) + AED 200 (our commission) = AED 2,500 total. We create an AR invoice in Sage showing "Customer owes AED 2,500".

  • Upfront: Customer pays full amount immediately
  • Split Payments: Customer pays in installments (monthly/quarterly)
  • Non-Upfront: Custom payment arrangements
  • Premium & Commission combined in one invoice
  • Discount credit notes if customer gets a discount
  • 3-step workflow: Open → Ready to Post → Posted to GL
View AR Invoice Lifecycle →

🏭 4. AP Invoice Lifecycle

What it does: Creates an invoice in Sage 300 showing what we owe the insurance company (Accounts Payable). This is the premium amount that we must pay to the insurance provider for the policy.

Real-world example: Customer's car insurance premium is AED 2,300. We create an AP invoice in Sage showing "We owe AED 2,300 to XYZ Insurance Company". (Note: Our AED 200 commission is kept by us and recorded separately in AR).

  • Upfront: Pay insurer immediately
  • Split Payments: Pay insurer in installments
  • Non-Upfront: Custom payment schedules to insurer
  • Vendor payment obligations to insurance providers
  • Vendor ID links to insurance_providers table
  • Entry types: STRAIGHT (new) or REVERSAL (corrections)
View AP Invoice Lifecycle →

💳 5. Payment Mapping & Apply Prepayment

What it does: Links (or "applies") the customer's payment receipt to their invoice, effectively closing the transaction. This tells Sage "the AED 2,500 the customer paid matches the AED 2,500 they owe - transaction complete!"

Real-world example: On Monday, customer paid AED 2,500 (receipt created). On Tuesday, we generated their AED 2,500 invoice. Now we "apply" Monday's receipt to Tuesday's invoice. Sage marks the invoice as "PAID" and the books balance.

  • Upfront: Match one receipt to one invoice
  • Split Payments: Match each installment receipt to invoice schedule
  • Non-Upfront: Custom matching logic
  • Zero price policies: Skip payment application (no money exchanged)
  • Historic data: Compatibility with pre-M2 migration data
View Payment Mapping →

📋 6. Main Lead & Send Update Processing

What it does: Orchestrates the complete booking workflow for both new policies (Main Lead) and policy changes (Send Update/Endorsements). This is the "master process" that calls all the other steps in the correct order.

Real-world example: Main Lead = Customer buys new car insurance (run all 6 steps: verify customer → create receipts → invoices → apply payments → complete). Send Update = Customer upgrades coverage 2 months later (create adjustment invoices and apply additional payment).

  • Main Lead: Initial policy booking with customer creation and full Sage workflow
  • Send Update - STRAIGHT: Simple premium increases and coverage additions
  • Send Update - REVERSAL: Full reversal and correction endorsement workflow
  • Send Update - CORRECTION: Error corrections and minor adjustments
  • Entry type determination (SCT_STRAIGHT, SCT_REVERSAL, SCT_CORRECTION)
  • Status tracking: quote_status_id vs send_update_logs.status
  • Payment differentiation: send_update_log_id NULL vs NOT NULL
  • BookPolicyOnSageJob and SendUpdateSageJob orchestration
  • Database tables: quotes, send_update_logs, payments, sage_processes
  • Integration with customer creation, receipts, invoices, and payment application
View Main Lead & Send Update Processing →

🔗 7. Embedded Product Booking (MDX, RDX, ECB)

What it does: Books add-on products (extras) that customers purchase along with their main policy. These are separate mini-policies that go through their own simplified Sage booking process.

Real-world example: Customer buys car insurance (main policy) + Driver Medical Cover (MDX add-on). Main policy books first using the full 6-step process. Then MDX books separately using a simplified 4-step embedded product process.

  • Three product types with distinct API methods (viewQuotePolicy vs issuePolicy)
  • MDX & RDX: Driver and rider medical cover using viewQuotePolicy with EmbeddedProduct call type
  • ECB: Excess cashback benefit using issuePolicy with insurance_provider_id filtering
  • Product eligibility validation and LOB support (Car, Bike)
  • Automatic booking triggers during policy booking (TAP gateway)
  • EP-specific AR receipt and invoice creation (4-step process)
  • BookEmbeddedProductOnSageJob with WithoutOverlapping middleware
  • Status management: READY_FOR_SAGE → QUEUED → COMPLETED/FAILED
  • Database: embedded_transactions, sage_embedded_product_statuses
  • Note: AP receipt creation currently disabled for EP
View Embedded Product Booking →

📄 8. Proforma Payment Request

What it does: Generates preliminary invoice documents (proforma) sent to customers to request payment before the actual policy invoice is issued. It serves as a payment requisition tool that helps track payment progress.

Real-world example: Customer wants to pay later instead of upfront. We select "Proforma Payment Request" as payment method, generate a PDF showing AED 2,500 due, and send it to the customer. When they're ready to pay, they use this document to initiate payment.

  • Payment requisition before formal invoice issuance
  • PDF generation with comprehensive payment breakdown
  • Support for main leads and send updates (endorsements)
  • Version tracking for multiple document generations
  • Upfront and split payment scenarios
  • Broker vs insurer collection type handling
  • Discount application and zero-price policy support
  • Secure document storage and delivery via S3
  • Permission-based access control
View Payment Proforma Documentation →

🔍 9. Sage Failed Processes Management

What it does: Provides a comprehensive interface to monitor, troubleshoot, and resolve policy bookings or endorsements that encountered errors during Sage 300 integration. Critical for maintaining financial accuracy.

Real-world example: A car insurance policy was sold and customer paid AED 3,000, but the Sage booking failed at the "Create AR Invoice" step due to a duplicate customer error. The policy appears in the failed processes list where admins can see the exact error, fix the customer record in Sage, and retry the booking.

  • Comprehensive failed process listing with detailed filtering
  • Multi-dimensional filters: provider, quote type, date range, main lead vs send update
  • Complete payment and commission breakdown for each failure
  • Extracted IMCRM-friendly error messages from Sage responses
  • One-click copy functionality for error details and API endpoints
  • CSV export with all filters applied for reporting
  • Optimized query performance with eager loading and read replicas
  • Permission-based access via SAGE_PROCESS_ISSUE_MANAGEMENT
  • Clickable links to quote/send update detail pages
  • Comprehensive troubleshooting guide with common error scenarios
